## Summary

BG1190 was rejected at round 2, the review cap, by qa-seat-BG1190-r1, so it was carried as a known issue rather than reviewed again. The findings still open: BLOCKING: [regression] A SIGTERM or Ctrl-C to the caller's group no longer reaches a verifier's own verifiers when that verifier is itself a `run_verifier` caller. That is the release verify lane's common shape: a Verify line runs pytest over tests that call `run_verifier`, or runs verify\_ac.py, gate.py or sprint.py on a fixture. The reaper sends SIGKILL (verify\_ac.py:499), which the inner caller cannot catch. Its own reaper never runs, and the verifiers it started in their own sessions run on with no ceiling, because the parent that enforced it is dead. Executed with an outer caller whose one verifier is `exec python3 inner.py`, which itself runs `run_verifier("shell ... sleep 8 & wait")`, then `kill -TERM -- -<outer pgid>`. Base 13b1d560 left 0 of the inner verifier's processes alive 1 s after the caller died by SIGTERM. HEAD 152fb52d left 2 (the shell and its sleep, reparented). Round 1's first blocking regression is therefore MOVED one level down, not closed. The same SIGKILL also discards each verifier's own signal handling. A verifier `trap 'echo cleaned > f' INT TERM HUP` ran its trap at base under SIGTERM (1/1), under SIGINT on a two-thread pool (2/2) and under SIGHUP (1/1). At HEAD it never did (0/1, 0/2, 0/1). So a pytest or unittest verifier interrupted by Ctrl-C no longer runs its KeyboardInterrupt teardown, and it leaves behind its temporary directories and the children it started in their own sessions. Fix sketch: forward the received signal to each live group, which is what base delivered, and escalate to SIGKILL only for a group still alive after a short bound. Pin it with this nested probe (the inner group is gone after a SIGTERM to the outer group) and with a trapping verifier whose trap runs; [new] The changelog's "so the children it spawned no longer keep running to load the verifiers after it" (changelog.d/BG1190.md:3-4) is over-stated for a verifier that is itself a `run_verifier` caller. On a timeout, the group's SIGKILL (verify\_ac.py:474) kills the inner caller before it can reap, and its verifiers, each in a session of its own, run on. Executed with an outer ceiling of 2 s and an inner verifier `sleep 8 & wait`: 2 inner processes were alive 1 s after the verdict at HEAD, as at base, so this is not a regression. AC1 holds as written, because the leak is outside the verifier's own group. A SIGTERM a short grace before the SIGKILL in `_kill_group` would let the nested caller pass the kill on. Not blocking; [new] Dropping "SIGHUP" from `_install_reaper`'s list (verify\_ac.py:515) passes all 10 tests in test\_verify\_timeout\_reaps.py. The mutant is not equivalent: a SIGHUP to the caller's group then leaves the verifier running, where HEAD as shipped leaves 0 live and the caller exits -1. The changelog claims SIGHUP. Not blocking; [new] Skipping the prior handler so the process dies by the default disposition (`if callable(prior)` changed to `if False` at verify\_ac.py:501) also passes all 10 tests. The SIGINT test reads -2 either way, because an uncaught KeyboardInterrupt also exits -2. The mutant is not equivalent: it skips a caller's own Python handler, such as migrate.py:1135, which turns SIGTERM into `sys.exit` so its bytecode guard is removed, and it skips every `except` and `finally` in the caller. A test whose prior handler writes a file would kill it. The test's docstring names only the return-without-calling variant (killed: rc 0 against -2). Not blocking; [new] Round-1 finding stands: BG1190 duplicates the open BG1112, and neither BG1190's artefact nor its changelog names it (grep count 0). BG1112 is still Open, and `_kill_group` is still a third group-kill helper beside mutation.py:1010-1018 and verify\_ac.py:2248. Not blocking; [new] Round-1 finding stands: Affects still declares scripts/tests/test\_verify\_ac.py, whose diff in scope is BG1223's `_side_by_side` wait and two tests, not this unit's. That change was not judged here. Not blocking. Re-rulings of round 1, each by execution: the SIGTERM to the caller's group is CLOSED for a direct verifier (HEAD leaves 0 live and the caller exits -15. With `_install_reaper()` removed, the round-1 state, 3 group members stay live and the test fails) and MOVED for a nested one (above). Ctrl-C on worker threads is CLOSED: a two-verifier pool exits -2 in 0.12 s at HEAD against 8.80 s at base for the same fixture, and leaving SIGINT out of the list fails the test. The unpinned SIGKILL is CLOSED: SIGTERM for SIGKILL at verify\_ac.py:474 fails the outright-kill test at 4.02 s against its 2.5 s bound. The unpinned reap wait is CLOSED: removing it fails the same test at 3.0 s. The other mutants were also killed: `_LIVE_GROUPS.add` removed, the handler's killpg removed, no re-raise, installing over SIG\_IGN, and `_LIVE_GROUPS.discard` removed. The tree was byte-identical after mutation. Claim inventory: TRUE are the SIGKILL, reap and 2 s wait, the main-thread handler that hands the signal on, an ignored signal staying ignored, the sub-second Ctrl-C on a pool, the waiting-thread KeyboardInterrupt and the no-killpg fallback. OVER-STATED is "the children no longer keep running" (above). UNVERIFIABLE is "Measured through `verify_ac.py run`": I measured through `run_verifier` in a driver, not the CLI. AC1 is met: `verify_ac.py run --id BG1190 --dry-run` reads pass=1, and the module passed 10 of 10 in 16.1 s at load 19.3
