## Summary

Split from BG0673 at its second plan review (RUN-01M2JA6J, 2026-09-15), which judged the whole at 13 points. BG0673 wires the gate: the record and review verbs, the transition call, the repair-plan kind and a clear test-plan ledger. This unit carries what the wired gate needs to be usable and honest: every round of a plan kept under the unit id (so the design threshold counts rounds on one unit), the reviewed findings' fingerprint written as the verdict's brief provenance (so an independent APPROVE of a revised round retires the earlier REJECT), a whole-plan pin that a post-approval re-record invalidates, and plan-author independence (a reviewer who is the plan's recorded author never satisfies the gate). Without it, a unit whose repair plan was rejected once can never close, and an approved plan can be rewritten and keep its approval.

## Steps to Reproduce

Measured by the round-2 QA seat on the planned BG0673 build: record a plan, review REJECT (bob), re-record, review APPROVE (bob) -> `plan_reviewed` ok=False and `verdict_for(kind=repair-plan)` = REJECT, because `review_repair_plan` writes no brief. Three rounds on one unit id are accepted where RP1..RP3 is refused, because each round overwrites `<unit>.json`. An approved plan re-recorded with an unreviewed approach still passes, because the pin hashes findings only.

## Proposed Fix

Carry BG0673's former AC4, AC5, AC9 and AC10 (now AC1-AC4) with their Test Plan rows: rounds stored per unit, never overwritten; the findings fingerprint in the Brief cell retiring the REJECT; the whole-plan hash in the approval pin; the reviewer compared with the plan's recorded author as well as the row's. Depends on BG0673.

Implementer notes: `RepairPlanPinTests` records plans under one id and must stay green once rounds are stored separately. `DesignDecisionTests` records under RP1, RP2 and RP3, three unit ids: the regression it guards, and must keep guarding unchanged, is that storing rounds per unit still counts rounds ACROSS different unit ids. `critic.py` is not touched: the Brief cell holds the first 12 hex characters of `findings_fingerprint` (which returns 16), the exact shape `critic.py record --brief` already accepts, and that 12-hex check is not widened. If the whole-plan pin renames the `findings-hash=` token, `UntokenedVerdictPinTests` (BG0267) asserts that token in its refusal and is updated to the new token, never loosened to accept either.

Sequencing with BG0672 (same run): BG0678 is built after BG0672, whose `critic.py record` marks `unmatched` beside any brief no `critic.py brief` render matches, and whose `_brief_key` stops keying a marked row. Unless BG0672's matcher recognises the repair-plan kind (a repair-plan row whose Brief is the first 12 hex of its plan's findings fingerprint is matched), every `critic.py record --kind repair-plan` row is marked, so a CLI-recorded repair-plan REJECT has no key and no APPROVE can retire it. This unit therefore records every REJECT its fixtures expect to be retired through `repair_plan.py review` only; AC1's `critic.py record` rows are APPROVEs judged on independence, which the marker does not change. The findings fingerprint goes in the verdict's Brief cell - the key every round of one unit's plan shares, and so the only one under which a revised round's APPROVE retires the round-one REJECT - and the whole-plan hash (findings and entries) goes in the approval pin; swapping them is AC2's fourth mutant.
