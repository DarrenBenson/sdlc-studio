### BG0631

**A repair row was joined to the rejection it answers by DATE alone.** `_REPAIR_COLS` carried
neither the phase nor the rejection, and `repairs_for`took no phase, so`repair_state` selected
rows on `verdict_date` equality across both review phases.

The two shapes originally filed both pass on HEAD and are not the defect: `repair_state` loops
per rejection, and `resolve_finding` separates rejections whose findings read differently. What
still failed is **text collision** - the same finding raised in both phases on one date - and
that is the ordinary shape rather than an exotic one, because a plan-review finding surviving
into delivery is what a review round normally produces. Reproduced: a delivery repair made
`repair_state(..., "plan-review")`read`complete` with nothing outstanding.

It stopped being a reporting nuisance when BG0629 landed. A plan-review REJECT is now retired by
its repair, so a delivery repair discharging a plan-review rejection opens a gate that should
have stayed shut.

**What changed.** The ledger carries a `Phase` column, appended so rows written before it still
parse with the field absent. `repairs_for` takes a phase and filters on it. A row that names its
phase is taken at its word; a legacy row is attributed only where the date is UNAMBIGUOUS -
exactly one phase rejected that day - and answers neither phase where both did. Guessing which
one a legacy row meant would be the record made prettier rather than truer, which this project
refused once already when a backfill cited cross-seat approvals as its evidence.

`unattributable_repairs` reports what the change cannot place: **13 legacy rows** on the live
ledger, each naming the unit, the date and the phases the date collides across. Reported rather
than absorbed, because a reader counting repairs must be able to see how many could not be
placed.

One consequence, recorded because it changes an existing message: the test-plan gate now refuses
one step earlier for a collision. The state is `none`rather than`partial`, so it says "no
repair is recorded against it" instead of naming an outstanding finding - the finding was only
ever visible there because the delivery repair was being counted as a partial answer to it.

**The population that moved, named.** Before this change the delivery lane read 7 non-conformant
units; it now reads 1. The seven were US0569-US0576, whose rejections were unanswered since
2026-07-31 and are resolved in this run by recorded repairs pointing at where the work went
(BG0638, and BG0461 which had already fixed the rest).

**US0674 moved the other way and is reported rather than repaired.** Its three repair rows are
dated 2026-08-24, carry no phase, and both phases rejected that day - so the date cannot place
them. Their closures do answer the delivery rejection in substance (they name the release-notes
finding verbatim), but they do not resolve MECHANICALLY: they are written in the `#1 ->` ordinal
form against a list that has since changed, and some fragments are the pre-BG0618 truncation.
Attributing them anyway would be guessing, which AC4 exists to refuse. So US0674 stands
non-conformant with the reason recorded here, rather than being given a phase nobody can show it
had.

Legacy attribution reads the row's own CLOSURES where the date is ambiguous, not the date alone.
The first cut used dates only and made genuinely repaired units read unrepaired - a false
negative in the direction of accusing finished work, which is the opposite of the failure this
unit exists to fix.
